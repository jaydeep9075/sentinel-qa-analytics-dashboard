1. Executive Summary

Your team already runs tests and already produces results. What's missing isn't more testing — it's a fast, trustworthy way to turn those results into an answer a leader can act on. TR-Insight is an AI-powered test analytics dashboard that sits on top of the test results you already have and answers plain-English questions about them — instantly, with a chart, and with a release call you can defend.

It runs entirely inside your own environment — nothing about your releases, defects, or customer-sensitive test data leaves your infrastructure. It is deployed with a single command and starts answering questions the same day.

2. The Problem TR-Insight Solves

Every QA organization running at any real scale eventually runs into the same five problems:

  • No defensible answer, under time pressure. A release is hours away and nobody can say, with evidence, whether it's safe to ship.
  • Reporting is manual labor. Every status update starts with someone exporting results into a spreadsheet by hand.
  • One result, two stories. The same failure means one thing to leadership and something else entirely to the engineer who owns the suite — there's no shared, role-appropriate framing.
  • Flaky tests hide in the noise until they take down a release nobody saw coming.
  • Tribal knowledge is a single point of failure. Insight depends on the one person who knows where the data lives — and they're on leave the week it matters.
"We can't tell the CTO whether we're safe to ship without someone spending an hour building a slide first." — the exact situation TR-Insight is built to eliminate.

5. What You Can Do With It

  • Ask in plain English — e.g. "is the payment module stable?" — and get a direct, sourced answer, not a raw data dump.
  • Get charts and dashboards on demand — presentation-ready visuals, ready to drop into a status update or a steering call, no design effort required.
  • See trends and patterns automatically — recurring failures, regressions and flaky tests found, explained, and ranked, with a recommendation on what to fix first.
  • Get answers shaped for the reader — leadership gets risk and release framing, engineers get failing tests and technical detail, all from the same underlying source of truth.
  • Defend a go/no-go call — a clear release-readiness verdict, with the evidence one click away if someone pushes back.
  • Watch a test run live — follow execution as it happens, so problems surface during the run itself instead of in tomorrow's report.

6. Bring the Data You Already Have

There is no migration project and no rebuild required. TR-Insight connects, in a few clicks, to what your team is already producing:

  • Automation reports (from your existing test frameworks)
  • Spreadsheet exports and Excel workbooks maintained by hand
  • Existing databases
  • Internal reporting services, via API
  • Live test runs, streamed while they're still executing

Four practical routes get data in — a watched drop-box folder, a direct upload endpoint for CI systems that can't share a filesystem, a manual "Add Build" action inside the dashboard, or a one-shot bulk import. Whichever route is used, the result is the same: a new build appears in the dashboard, typically within seconds, with no restart or manual refresh needed.

7. Why AI, and Why It's Safe to Rely On

Why AI belongs here

  • Test data is large, repetitive, and dull to read line-by-line — exactly the work people postpone, and exactly what AI does instantly.
  • It reads every result, not the sample someone had time to look at, so nothing quietly gets skipped.
  • It explains what happened in language a non-technical stakeholder can act on, with no translation layer required.
  • It ranks what actually matters — the one failure blocking the release, ahead of the twenty that don't.

Why the answers can be trusted

Charts and figures are not free-form AI output. The system deterministically parses what chart is being asked for, has the AI write only the underlying data query, and then validates and downgrades the result if the data can't actually support the requested chart form. In plain terms: the AI decides what to look up, but a fixed, testable process decides what gets drawn — so the same question reliably produces the same kind of answer, and visuals can't silently misrepresent the underlying numbers.

8. Cost You Can Predict

  • Repeat and follow-up questions reuse work already done instead of paying for the same computation twice.

9. Data Security & Ownership

TR-Insight runs inside your own environment — on your own server, VM, or cloud account, brought up with a single command. Release information, defect detail, and customer-sensitive test data never leave your infrastructure by default, which keeps security review short and procurement simple compared to a SaaS product that requires sending your data to a third party for storage.

  • Accounts are protected with industry-standard authentication and password hashing; every deployment gets its own unique credentials — there is no shared factory password across installs.
  • Access is scoped by team ("workspace") — a person only sees the builds that belong to their own team unless they hold an administrator or leadership role.
  • Every significant action — logins, account changes, permission changes, deletions — is recorded in a permanent audit trail that leadership and compliance can review at any time.
  • The one place data does travel is to the AI provider your organization chooses (e.g., Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic, or a fully local/offline model via Ollama) — and only the specific question and the minimal supporting data it needs, never the full dataset.

10. Getting Started — What It Takes

Standing TR-Insight up does not require a services engagement or a lengthy implementation project:

  1. One setup command brings up the entire application (dashboard + backend) on a server, VM, or laptop.
  2. The first login is a one-time administrator account, which is immediately forced to set a real password.
  3. The administrator picks an AI provider from a short list (or connects a fully private, offline model) — this can also be finished later without restarting anything.
  4. The first set of test results is dropped in, and it appears in the dashboard within about 30 seconds.
  5. Everyone else requests an account through a simple self-service sign-up, approved by an administrator into the right team.

From a cold start to a working, populated dashboard is typically a same-day effort for a technical team member — not a multi-week rollout.

SDET / QA EngineerFull read and import access, scoped to their own team's results

A "team" (called a workspace) is simply a boundary around which builds a person can see — assigning someone to a new team is all it takes to create one; there's no separate setup step. New sign-ups sit in a pending queue until an administrator approves them into the correct team and role, so access is always a deliberate decision, never automatic.

Independently of account access, each person can also choose which "voice" the AI answers in — an executive risk summary versus full technical failure detail — without that choice changing what data they're allowed to see.
